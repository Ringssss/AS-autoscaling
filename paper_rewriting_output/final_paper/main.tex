\title{\Large \bf AgentShift: Execution Mobility for Suspended LLM Agents}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LLM agents alternate between model inference and external tools. After an LLM
turn completes, an agent may wait for a tool while its accumulated prefix
remains cached on one serving engine. This \emph{suspended-but-warm} state
creates a placement conflict: sticky routing preserves the prefix but
concentrates returning agents, whereas rerouting relieves load but reconstructs
history. Existing routing, retention, offload, and active-request migration do
not transfer a suspended agent's warm state together with its right to execute.

AgentShift makes suspended executions mobile across identical LLM engines. It
separates durable continuation from reconstructable KV state, atomically
installs the completed prefix on a destination, and commits an epoch-fenced
ownership handoff during the blocked interval. A source shadow provides fast
recovery until the destination produces its first token.

We implement AgentShift in SGLang and evaluate Qwen3 models with tensor
parallelism up to four. At a 32K-token prefix and a 500~ms gap, AgentShift
matches the sticky latency floor while reducing post-tool latency by 24.04$\times$
over rerouting and 2.41$\times$ over the same transfer started on return. In an
eight-agent 32K burst, it relocates half the owners with full prefix hits and is
7.63$\times$ faster than rerouting. Warm-pool scale-out and scale-in are
3.71$\times$ and 6.43$\times$ faster than semantic rerouting. Within our fault
model, epoch fencing prevents double advancement and source-shadow recovery is
10.9$\times$ faster than reconstruction.
\end{abstract}
